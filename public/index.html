Twenty‑Eight

Twenty-Eight

Four players, partners sitting opposite. You are South; North is your partner. Dealing and play go counter-clockwise — to the right.

Cards and points

32 cards. In every suit: J > 9 > A > 10 > K > Q > 8 > 7. J = 3, 9 = 2, A = 1, 10 = 1, the rest nothing — 28 points in all.

Bidding

Four cards each, then bidding. The player on the dealer's right must open, at the table minimum (16, or 14 as a house rule) — unless those four cards hold no points at all, in which case they may demand a redeal. Each player then raises or passes; a pass is final. To bid over your own partner you must bid at least 20.

The hidden trump

The winner picks a card from their hand and lays it face down: its suit is trump, and nobody else knows it — not even their partner. Then four more cards each.

Second round, doubling

With all eight cards seen, the bidder may raise to 24 or more, and then the partner may raise above that — a partner who does takes over the contract and sets a new trump. Next the opponents may double (game points ×2), and the bidder may then redouble (×4).

Play

The player on the dealer's right leads. Follow suit if you can; the highest card of the led suit wins. When you cannot follow you may discard, or call for trump: the face-down card is shown and returns to the bidder's hand, and you must then play a trump if you have one. From then on trumps beat everything — but a trump-suit card played before the reveal was only a discard.

The bidder may not lead the hidden trump suit while holding anything else. If trump is led, the bidder may not reveal. If they cannot follow another suit, they either discard from a non-trump suit or reveal and trump. If trump is still hidden after seven tricks it is turned up for the last.

Pair

The King and Queen of trump in one hand. Once trump is revealed and your side has won a trick since, show it on your turn: it lowers your own side's contract by 4 (not below the table minimum), or raises the opponents' by 4 (up to 28).

Thani

After all eight cards, the bidder may declare Thani: they will win every trick alone. Their partner sits out, there is no trump, the bidder leads, and a single lost trick ends it. +4 if made, −5 if not.

Scoring

Only the bidding side's score moves. Bid 16–19: +1 / −2. Bid 20–24: +2 / −3. Bid 25–28: +3 / −4. Doubling multiplies it. First side to +6 wins the match; a side that sinks to −6 loses it.

Cot: taking all eight tricks scores double. A side that has lost every trick so far may concede; the winners take the single score, or play on — double if they take every trick, but they lose double if they drop one.

Redeals

Also redealt: when one player holds all four Jacks; when the trump turns out to be one the opponents were never dealt; and (house rule) when any eight-card hand has no points.

House rules

No Jack discard: when you cannot follow suit, you may not throw a Jack away (the Jack of a revealed trump may still trump). Joker — not traditional, a house variant: it replaces the 7♣, is worth nothing, may be played at any time, and once trump is revealed it is the highest trump; before that it cannot win a trick and cannot be the face-down card.
